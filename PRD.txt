One-pager: AI-Powered Mobile-First POS Web Application
1. TL;DR
A modern, mobile-first web-based Point of Sale (POS) system designed for small to medium-sized food and beverage businesses. This application combines instant checkout capabilities, AI-powered features, comprehensive inventory management, and offline functionality to deliver a seamless transaction experience comparable to platforms like Gojek and Shopee Food. The system supports both real-time and retroactive transaction recording, enabling business owners to maintain accurate records even when entering past sales data.

Target Users: Restaurant owners, café operators, food stall vendors, and small retail businesses requiring a flexible, mobile-accessible POS solution.

Why It Matters: Most existing POS systems are either too complex, desktop-dependent, or lack the flexibility needed by modern mobile-first businesses. This solution bridges that gap with an intuitive interface, offline capabilities, and AI assistance for inventory and sales optimization.

5. User experience
Primary User Journey - Completing a Sale
User opens the web app on mobile device (loads instantly via PWA)
Dashboard shows quick access to "New Sale" button prominently displayed
User taps "New Sale" and sees product grid with search bar at top
User either searches or browses categories to add products to cart
Each product tap adds to cart with visual confirmation (number badge increases)
Cart icon shows real-time total amount
User taps cart icon to review order (shows product list, quantities, subtotal)
User selects payment method from bottom sheet (Cash/QRIS/Other)
For QRIS: system generates QR code; user receives automatic confirmation when paid
For Cash: user enters amount received, system calculates change
Receipt is automatically generated (option to print or send digitally)
Transaction completes, user returns to new sale screen
Secondary Journey - Recording Past Transaction
User accesses "Past Transactions" from menu
Taps "Add Past Sale" button
System shows date/time picker allowing user to select when sale occurred
User follows standard product selection flow
System tags transaction as backdated with clear timestamp
Transaction appears in reports for the correct historical date
Edge Cases & UI Notes
Offline Mode:

Clear visual indicator when offline (orange banner at top)
All features work normally except QRIS auto-verification
Pending sync indicator shows number of unsynced transactions
Auto-sync when connection restored with progress indicator
Split Payments:

User can select "Split Payment" option before finalizing
Interface allows adding multiple payment methods with amounts
System validates total matches order amount before proceeding
Inventory Alerts:

Non-intrusive notification badge on inventory icon when items are low
Warning shown when selling product with zero or low stock (doesn't block sale)
Option to continue sale anyway or adjust stock first
Cart Management:

"Hold Order" button saves current cart with unique reference number
Held orders appear in sidebar for quick recall
Maximum 10 held orders with visual indication of order time
Product Modifiers:

Quick access to common modifiers (add-ons, sizes) via button below product
Modifier selections shown clearly in cart with individual pricing
Role-Based Views:

Cashier role: Cannot access reports, settings, or user management
Manager role: Can access all features except user deletion and role changes
Owner role: Full access to all features
Viewer role: Read-only access to reports and inventory
Mobile Optimization:

Bottom navigation for primary actions (always accessible)
Swipe gestures for common actions (swipe cart items to delete)
Large, finger-friendly touch targets (minimum 44x44px)
Minimal text input; prefer selection and buttons
Pull-to-refresh for reports and inventory lists
6. Narrative
Morning at "Kopi Santai"

Maya arrives at her café at 6 AM, pulls out her phone while setting up, and opens her POS web app. The dashboard immediately shows yesterday's numbers: Rp 3.2 million in sales, Rp 1.1 million profit—a new record. She smiles, remembering the system's AI suggestion last week to create a breakfast combo deal that's now a bestseller.

The morning rush begins. A customer orders an Americano and croissant. Maya taps both items on her phone—two taps, done. "Cash or QRIS?" she asks. "QRIS," the customer replies. Maya taps the QRIS button, a QR code appears instantly on her screen. The customer scans it, and within two seconds, her phone vibrates with a confirmation. The receipt is already generated. Total time: 15 seconds.

Around 9 AM, the internet goes down—something that used to panic her. Now she just notices a small orange indicator at the top of her screen. No problem. She continues taking orders seamlessly. The system stores everything locally and will sync automatically when the connection returns.

At 11 AM, her employee Andi arrives for the lunch shift. He logs in with his cashier credentials and immediately starts serving customers. Maya notices the milk is running low—an alert popped up on her inventory screen. The AI suggests she'll need to reorder by tomorrow based on sales patterns. She makes a mental note and adds the milk purchase to next week's shopping list.

During the quiet afternoon period, Maya remembers she forgot to record three cash sales from yesterday when the internet was down and her phone died. She opens "Past Transactions," selects yesterday's date and evening time, and quickly enters those sales. Her reports are now accurate again.

By 4 PM, Maya's curious about her profit margin on that popular breakfast combo. She opens the reports section, selects "Product Performance," and immediately sees detailed breakdowns: the combo costs her Rp 18,000 to make (the app calculated this from her ingredient costs), sells for Rp 35,000, giving her a 48% profit margin—her best performer. The AI suggests she could increase the price by Rp 2,000 without affecting demand based on market data.

She creates a bundled "Afternoon Delight" deal—coffee and cake for Rp 40,000. The system automatically calculates the bundled pricing and tracks it as a separate product. Within an hour, she's sold four of them.

Before closing at 8 PM, Maya reviews the day's complete picture: 87 transactions, Rp 3.8 million in revenue, Rp 1.4 million in profit, 23 transactions via QRIS, 64 cash. She notices Friday afternoons are becoming increasingly busy—the AI chart shows a 25% uptick over the past month. She decides to schedule an extra staff member for next Friday.

She locks up, confident that every transaction is recorded, every expense is tracked, and tomorrow she'll start with clear, actionable insights. The system handled everything from instant checkouts to complex inventory calculations, all from her phone, working seamlessly whether she was online or offline.
